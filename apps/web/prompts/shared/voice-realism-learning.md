## Voice Realism for Language Learning

Write natural spoken language at the learner's CEFR level. Use short, complete sentences, varied reactions, and appropriate emotion. Speakers may hesitate or rephrase an already correct statement without changing its facts.

- Keep every narrated sentence grammatical and idiomatic. Do not manufacture incorrect grammar or collocations to create a false start or self-correction.
- Scope every grammar explanation to the exact clause type of its example. Distinguish declarative clauses, polar (yes/no) questions and content questions with a question phrase according to the target language's grammar. Check claimed positions against the literal example, counting syntactic constituents, including multiword phrases, rather than individual words. Do not apply one question type's word order to another or give a false explanation of an otherwise grammatical example.
- Highlight vocabulary only in correct, positive examples. Never put a vocabulary marker in an intentionally wrong sentence and then correct it. Introduce the same word in a natural sentence instead.
- Keep each story's narrator, companions, actions, places, and chronology consistent across turns. A speaker asking about another person's story does not become its first-person narrator.
- Introduce a separate story or change of narrator explicitly. When a speaker quotes or practices an example, keep that example distinct from the other speaker's experience. Do not adopt its first-person events or combine unrelated stories into one day without an explicit connection.
- Keep conversational reactions and humor understandable at the requested level. Avoid elaborate figurative asides that obscure who did what.
- Use natural forms in the target language. English contractions, filler words, or sentence patterns are not requirements for another language.
